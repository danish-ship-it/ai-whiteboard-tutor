# Chalk for children aged 10–12

Part of the [learning-UX report](learning-ux-report.md); full references are there. Lines marked *weak evidence* rest on practice or inference rather than direct experiments.

## What is different at this age

- **Sustained attention is close to adult levels by about 12, but drops faster under heavy load** (Betts et al. 2006 [doi](https://doi.org/10.1080/09297040500350938)).
- **Learner control helps.** In a study with 11–12-year-olds learning from an animation, giving them pause or segment control reduced load and improved test performance (Hasler, Kersten & Sweller 2007 [doi](https://doi.org/10.1002/acp.1345)).
- **Practice testing and prediction work; drawing and self-questioning prompts are still unreliable** before secondary school (Brod 2021 [doi](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09571-9)).
- **Competent readers**, so captions become optional rather than essential.

## Recommended settings

| Parameter | Recommendation | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Lesson length | 2–3 min, 3–4 steps | Guo et al. 2014; the exact figure is *weak evidence* |
| Step length | 20–30 s; optional pause at step ends, on by default | Rey et al. 2019; Hasler et al. 2007 |
| Board text | At most 6–8 labels, short words or symbols; minimum ≈ 80 units (≈ 18 px on a phone) | Legge & Bigelow 2011; the size figure is *weak evidence* |
| Colour | Default inks; two hue codes per scene; keep each quantity in its ink across the lesson | Ozcelik et al. 2009; simulation in the main report |
| Narration pace | ≈ 135–150 words per minute; offer 0.75× and 1× | *Weak evidence*, practice-based |
| Tone | Friendly and curious ("Let's figure out why..."); use a real-world hook in the first 10 s | Personalisation: Ginns et al. 2013. The hook must be relevant to the content, not a seductive detail (Rey 2012). |
| Interactivity | One multiple-choice question per step (3 options); a prediction at the start; 2-item recall at the end | Szpunar et al. 2013; Adesope et al. 2017; Pan & Carpenter 2023 |
| Feedback | Immediate and explanatory ("Not quite: the hypotenuse is opposite the right angle"), with the board pointing to the answer | Wisniewski et al. 2020 |
| Motivation | Visible progress (outline checks, "3 / 4") and a sense of mastery; a "challenge question" option for fast learners | Ryan & Deci 2000; expertise reversal (Kalyuga et al. 2003) |
| Captions | On by default, togglable | Gernsbacher 2015 |

## Avatar at this age

The marker nib plus the voice indicator is enough. Agent benefits for K-12 exist but are small (Schroeder et al. 2013). The specific-pointing benefit is the part worth keeping (Li et al. 2019). A cartoon tutor risks seeming babyish to this age (*weak evidence*).

## Chalk changes for this band

1. A 10–12 preset: 3–4 steps, pause on, 0.75× available.
2. A step-end question plus end recall.
3. An optional "challenge" follow-up chip on Lesson complete ("Try one harder").
