# Chalk for children aged 6–9

Part of the [learning-UX report](learning-ux-report.md); full references are there. Lines marked *weak evidence* rest on practice or inference rather than direct experiments.

## What is different at this age

- **Sustained attention is still developing.** Children's ability to hold attention on a task improves markedly from 5 to 12, and performance falls faster when the task is demanding (Betts et al. 2006 [doi](https://doi.org/10.1080/09297040500350938)). The popular "2–5 minutes per year of age" rule has no solid source (*weak evidence*).
- **They are easily pulled off task by visual clutter** (Fisher, Godwin & Seltman 2014 [doi](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797614533801)).
- **Many are beginning readers.** Captions can help word recognition, especially for children at risk of reading difficulties (Linebarger, Piotrowski & Greenwood 2010 [doi](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9817.2009.01412.x)). They cannot rely on reading to follow the lesson, though, so the voice and the drawing must carry it.
- **Generative strategies that suit them:** practice testing and prediction work in lower primary school. Drawing and asking their own questions largely do not yet (Brod 2021 [doi](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09571-9)).

## Recommended settings

| Parameter | Recommendation | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Lesson length | 1.5–2 min, 3 steps | Guo et al. 2014 (shorter holds engagement); Betts 2006. The exact figure is *weak evidence*. |
| Step length | 15–25 s, auto-pause at each step end with a big "Next ▸" | Segmenting: Rey et al. 2019; learner pacing: Hasler et al. 2007 |
| Board text | At most 4–5 labels; single words or numbers; minimum ≈ 90 SVG units (≈ 20 px on a phone) | Reading speed fell with smaller text for 5–7-year-olds but not for 8–11-year-olds (Hughes & Wilkins 2000 [doi](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9817.00126); Legge & Bigelow 2011). The size figure is *weak evidence*. |
| Font | Instrument Sans works. Avoid all-caps words and condensed type. A single-storey "a" helps early readers (*weak evidence*). | Practice |
| Colour | Same paper board. Two hue-coded quantities at most. Slightly stronger tints for the current element are fine; no saturated backgrounds. | Fisher 2014; Wilms & Oberfeld 2018; colour-blind simulation in the main report |
| Narration pace | ≈ 120–135 words per minute, short sentences (≤ 12 words), a pause after each new idea | *Weak evidence*: practice-based, and consistent with load theory (Sweller et al. 2019) |
| Tone | Warm, concrete, everyday examples ("a pizza cut in half"); second person | Personalisation: Ginns, Martin & Marsh 2013 |
| Interactivity | One tap question after every step: 2 large picture or number choices, no typing, no timer. One "guess first" prediction at the start. | Brod 2021 (testing and predicting work at this age); Szpunar et al. 2013 |
| Feedback | Immediate. Right: "Yes! The longest side is across from the square corner." Wrong: show the correct answer on the board with the marker nib pointing, then move on. Praise the strategy, not the child ("You checked the corner first"). | Hattie & Timperley 2007; Mueller & Dweck 1998 [doi](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.75.1.33). The praise effect replicated poorly in Li & Bates 2019 [doi](https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0000669), so treat it as *weak evidence*. |
| Motivation | Short, clear win at the end ("You know 2 new words!"). No streaks or leaderboards. | Self-determination theory: competence (Ryan & Deci 2000 [doi](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.55.1.68)); gamification effects vary (Sailer & Homner 2020) |
| Captions | On by default, with the word highlight | Linebarger 2010 |

## Avatar at this age

Agent effects were larger for K-12 than for older learners (Schroeder, Adesope & Gilbert 2013 [doi](https://doi.org/10.2190/EC.49.1.a)). But children are the easiest to distract (Fisher 2014). Use the **marker nib that points** (Li et al. 2019). A simple non-human chalk character shown only at question moments is an optional experiment (*weak evidence*). Do not use a realistic face.

## Chalk changes for this band

1. Kid preset: 3 steps, auto-pause, bigger labels, slower voice.
2. A tap question after each step, plus a prediction at Ready.
3. Marker nib pointing at the current element.
4. Script rules: words of at most two syllables where possible, one idea per sentence, a concrete example before any symbol.
