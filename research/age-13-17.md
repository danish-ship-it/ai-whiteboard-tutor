# Chalk for teenagers aged 13–17

Part of the [learning-UX report](learning-ux-report.md); full references are there. Lines marked *weak evidence* rest on practice or inference rather than direct experiments.

## What is different at this age

- **The full range of generative strategies starts to work:** practice testing, prediction, explaining, drawing and self-questioning (Brod 2021 [doi](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09571-9)).
- **Status and respect drive motivation.** Adolescents respond badly to messages that feel controlling or condescending and well to ones that treat them as capable (Yeager, Dahl & Dweck 2018 [doi](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691617722620)).
- **Prior knowledge varies widely.** Detailed guidance that helps a novice can slow down a learner who already knows the topic (expertise reversal: Kalyuga et al. 2003 [doi](https://doi.org/10.1207/S15326985EP3801_4)).
- Many use lessons for homework or exam revision, so speed and skippability matter (*weak evidence*, inference).

## Recommended settings

| Parameter | Recommendation | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Lesson length | 2–4 min, 4 steps | Guo et al. 2014 |
| Step length | 25–40 s, continuous by default, with a pause option | Rey et al. 2019 |
| Board text | Default spec (72-unit minimum, ≤ 8 labels); equations in IBM Plex Mono | Spec §5; Legge & Bigelow 2011 |
| Colour | Default inks; two hue codes per scene. Avoid "kiddy" brightness: the current restrained palette suits teens. | Simulation in the main report; teen preference is *weak evidence* |
| Narration pace | ≈ 150–165 words per minute; 1× to 1.5× speed available | Murphy et al. 2022 (little cost up to 2× for young adults) |
| Tone | A respectful peer or older-sibling register. Plain and precise; no forced slang; explain why it matters ("This shows up in every physics problem with ramps") | Yeager et al. 2018; Ginns et al. 2013 |
| Interactivity | One question per step; a prediction before the key step ("What do you think a² + b² equals here?"); and "explain it back in one sentence" at the end, typed into the follow-up composer | Brod 2021 (explaining works from secondary school); Szpunar et al. 2013 |
| Feedback | Explanatory and competence-focused; show the score; allow "Why?" to jump into the follow-up chat | Wisniewski et al. 2020; Hattie & Timperley 2007 |
| Motivation | Autonomy: let them skip steps, change speed and ask anything. Offer a harder "exam-style" question on completion. | Ryan & Deci 2000; Kalyuga et al. 2003 |
| Captions | On by default, togglable | Gernsbacher 2015 |

## Avatar at this age

**No character.** A cartoon tutor risks the condescension that puts teens off (Yeager et al. 2018; applying it to avatars is *weak evidence*). An instructor face adds load without improving learning (Alemdag 2022 [doi](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10639-022-11532-4)). Use the marker nib and the voice indicator.

## Chalk changes for this band

1. A teen preset: 4 steps, normal pace, prediction prompt before the key step.
2. An "Explain it back" prompt on Lesson complete, answered in the existing follow-up composer with AI feedback.
3. A "Harder question" chip.
